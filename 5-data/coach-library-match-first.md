PadelIQ — Match-First Coaching Knowledge Library

Edition: Curated research working draft · 28 October 2026
Scope: 46 skills and competencies across seven categories
Purpose: Provide practical in-match awareness, actions, tactics and concise cues. Training drills are optional, not the default.
Status: Source-informed synthesis; not coach-validated, not automatically suitable for live AI retrieval without editorial checks.

How to read this library

Each entry contains Situation to recognise, Basic (notice), Intermediate (act), Advanced (adapt), Guardrail, Optional practice and References. These levels describe the complexity of the in-match task, not a player’s D/C/B/A rating. An advanced player might benefit from the Basic option. The coach normally proposes one suitable action, not all three.

Evidence markers:

• Direct — a specific relevant teaching point is explicitly present in a verified coaching or official source. The example wording is still our paraphrase/adaptation, not an independently tested drill.
• Indirect — related coaching principles support an approach, but the exact suggested match action is our inference.
• Limited — only general, rules-based or programme-level support was established; no detailed technical claim should be made without further research or expert review.

Important: A cited coaching source supports the principle, not every detail of the proposed Basic / Intermediate / Advanced application. PadelIQ must not claim it observed a player’s stroke. Match advice is conditional on ball quality, available time, the opponent and the partner; when those facts are unknown, ask or qualify. Record player statements, coach suggestions and outcomes separately. Privacy and enjoyment matter: don’t assign homework by default, save a plan without user approval, or pressure a player to improve after a fun match.

Research provenance and limitations

• The Padel School is the dominant open-access technical source in this edition; pages from a single academy are not independent corroboration.
• FIP’s official document index anchors rule-related topics. Verify the current underlying rulebook and competition format for any precise legal ruling.
• Peer-reviewed studies of fatigue, anxiety or match performance support broader context but do not prove that an individual player’s error has a particular cause or that a specific cue works.
• Optional practice is intentionally brief. The library is not a drill catalogue.
• Specialist shots and some groundstroke mechanics are marked Limited or Indirect rather than padded with fabricated specificity.

The coaching response rule

1. Respect the player’s intent (celebrate / reflect / advice / technical lesson / stop). No recommendation when none is wanted.
2. Connect the reported symptom to the relevant situation; ask one question only if it changes the recommendation.
3. Choose one realistic next-match focus at the lowest sufficient complexity; it may be to notice rather than change behaviour.
4. Explain the reason briefly; avoid fixed tactical rules or promises of success.
5. Suggest optional practice only when requested or when match actions have not helped and the player has an opportunity to practise.
6. Record the agreed plan only with player permission; retain failed and successful attempts for continuity.

Attacking shots

01. Serve — Saque

Evidence: Direct · References: S, R

• Situation to recognise: Are faults into net, long or wide; does the pattern change when rushed?
• Basic — Notice: Notice which target and serving rhythm produce your most repeatable first ball.
• Intermediate — Act: Use a comfortable target and finish the serve before moving into the court.
• Advanced — Adapt: Vary T/body/side-glass placements according to the returner’s response without sacrificing first-volley readiness.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not prescribe maximum power; check legality and whether advice applies to the first or second serve.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Between games, serve a few balls to a large safe target if court time allows.

02. Forehand volley — Volea de derecha

Evidence: Indirect · References: V, P

• Situation to recognise: Is it floating long, popping up, or netting; against what ball height and pace?
• Basic — Notice: On the next comfortable volley, notice whether the racket is ready before the ball arrives.
• Intermediate — Act: Against a fast ball, keep the action compact and choose a generous target instead of forcing a winner.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use an easy first volley to draw a weaker reply, then increase aggression only on the suitable next ball.
• Guardrail / exception: A short block is not the correct answer for every easy attacking volley.
• Optional practice (only when requested): With a partner, contrast soft and fast incoming balls to observe how much swing is needed.

03. Backhand volley — Volea de revés

Evidence: Indirect · References: V, P

• Situation to recognise: Does the error appear on body balls, low balls or shoulder-height balls?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether rushed backhand volleys happen before you have space to contact.
• Intermediate — Act: Use a compact, stable response to fast balls at the body, rather than a full swing.
• Advanced — Adapt: Distinguish a blocking volley from an attacking one using ball height, preparation and opponent position.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not assume every pop-up comes from an open racket face.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Ask a partner for varied speeds on the backhand side and compare responses.

04. Block volley — Volea bloqueada

Evidence: Direct · References: V

• Situation to recognise: Is the opponent hitting with pace while you are close to the net?
• Basic — Notice: Identify when you are defending at the net rather than attacking.
• Intermediate — Act: Prepare compactly against the hard hitter; redirect safely rather than adding more swing.
• Advanced — Adapt: Read opponents’ attacking posture and choose a low-risk block that helps the pair reset.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not treat blocking as failure or prescribe one fixed net distance.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Alternate hard and gentle feeds to practise recognise-block-versus-attack.

05. Drop volley — Dejada de volea

Evidence: Limited · References: A

• Situation to recognise: Are opponents deep enough for a soft volley to be difficult to retrieve?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether opponents are already near the net before considering the drop.
• Intermediate — Act: Choose the drop volley only from a controlled ball when opponents are displaced; otherwise retain placement control.
• Advanced — Adapt: Mix a conventional volley and occasional drop to exploit opponents’ depth without making the pattern predictable.
• Guardrail / exception: Insufficient public primary detail to prescribe exact racket contact or disguise mechanics.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Ask a coach to demonstrate touch/trajectory when a lesson is available.

06. Basic overhead — Remate básico

Evidence: Direct · References: O, B

• Situation to recognise: Is the lob short, central or deep; can you contact comfortably?
• Basic — Notice: Call the lob’s depth to yourself before committing to an overhead.
• Intermediate — Act: Choose a controlled overhead only when you can get balanced; use a defensive recovery if too deep.
• Advanced — Adapt: Balance controlled placement, opponent positions and court recovery rather than treating overhead as a winner attempt.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not force overhead contact when retreating off balance.
• Optional practice (only when requested): With a partner, call out ‘short / playable / deep’ as lobs arrive.

07. Bandeja — Bandeja

Evidence: Direct · References: B, O, M

• Situation to recognise: Does it fail on deeper lobs, during retreat, or despite balanced contact?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you turn early and meet the lob in front.
• Intermediate — Act: On a playable lob, control depth and recover court position rather than hitting a smash-like winner.
• Advanced — Adapt: Vary middle/corner depth to constrain return options; only advance when your shot creates recovery time.
• Guardrail / exception: Not all bandejas should be hit hard; not every deep lob is playable overhead.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Practise a few calm overheads with early turn and recovery if a partner can feed.

08. Víbora — Víbora

Evidence: Indirect · References: O, A

• Situation to recognise: Is the lob comfortable enough to attack with controlled slice?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise the types of lobs on which you are fully set.
• Intermediate — Act: Choose a víbora only when in position; otherwise maintain net control with a safer overhead.
• Advanced — Adapt: Target the opponent’s awkward rebound or corner and recover for the weak reply, not automatic winner.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not prescribe a víbora merely because the player is advanced.
• Optional practice (only when requested): If requested, alternate bandeja and víbora on manageable lob feeds.

09. Smash — Remate

Evidence: Direct · References: O, P

• Situation to recognise: Was the lob genuinely short/high enough; did the miss occur with max power?
• Basic — Notice: Notice which attempted smashes were taken off balance or from deep positions.
• Intermediate — Act: On a comfortable short lob, favour controllable placement and pace over automatic full power.
• Advanced — Adapt: Select between controlled smash, overhead containment and defensive retreat based on ball and opponents.
• Guardrail / exception: Never assume an overhit smash is always a contact-point issue.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Try varying controlled power levels only in an appropriate training session.

10. Hook — Gancho

Evidence: Direct · References: H, O

• Situation to recognise: Does the lob arrive over the non-dominant shoulder and restrict a regular overhead?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise the unusual lob path; do not force an unfamiliar hook in a match.
• Intermediate — Act: Choose a comfortable existing overhead or let a deep ball rebound while learning the shot.
• Advanced — Adapt: If already proficient, choose gancho situationally for a specific high lob and court-retention target.
• Guardrail / exception: High technical complexity; no detailed self-taught mechanics from text-only symptoms.
• Optional practice (only when requested): With a qualified coach, examine lob direction and whether gancho is appropriate.

11. Topspin smash — Remate liftado

Evidence: Indirect · References: A, O

• Situation to recognise: Are the lob, timing and contact sufficient for controlled topspin?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise that a topspin smash is a specialist option, not a default overhead.
• Intermediate — Act: Use a reliable flat/controlled overhead when the contact is not suitable for topspin.
• Advanced — Adapt: Choose spin and rebound target based on the defenders’ position and court conditions.
• Guardrail / exception: No promise of x3 or return-to-own-side outcome from a generic swing cue.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Use specialist coached feeds for topspin mechanics if desired.

12. Smash out the side (x3) — Por tres

Evidence: Limited · References: R, A

• Situation to recognise: Is the lob and target suitable for a legal x3 attempt?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether the ball is a genuine finishing opportunity rather than forcing x3.
• Intermediate — Act: Prioritise a conventional controlled smash if the ball is not clearly attackable.
• Advanced — Adapt: Consider court geometry, wind, spin, rebound and opponents’ outside-court access before choosing x3.
• Guardrail / exception: Advanced outcome, not a universal skill milestone; verify local outside-play permissions.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Specialist coached practice only, when needed.

13. Smash out the back (x4) — Por cuatro

Evidence: Limited · References: R, A

• Situation to recognise: Is contact close and high enough for this trajectory?
• Basic — Notice: Notice when trying x4 turns a playable overhead into an error.
• Intermediate — Act: Prefer a safe finishing placement if the contact position is imperfect.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use x4 only on suitable attackable balls, considering legal bounce and enclosure geometry.
• Guardrail / exception: Never imply x4 is always better than a controlled smash.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Specialist coached finishing-ball feeds only.

14. Bajada — Bajada

Evidence: Direct · References: M, G, A

• Situation to recognise: Does the back-glass rebound come high enough to attack, and are you balanced?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether the ball comes off the glass above your comfortable contact zone.
• Intermediate — Act: Attack only a comfortable rebound; on a low or hurried rebound, choose a safe defensive reply.
• Advanced — Adapt: Read opponent positions and change bajada depth/target while maintaining recovery.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not prescribe attacking bajada on every back-glass rebound.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Have a partner feed lobs and compare high/low rebounds if requested.

15. Drop shot — Dejada

Evidence: Indirect · References: A, P

• Situation to recognise: Is the player too deep, are opponents already close, and does the ball sit up?
• Basic — Notice: Observe opponents’ net distance before considering a drop.
• Intermediate — Act: Avoid attempting a delicate winner from a rushed back-court position; use a reliable deep ball instead.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use occasional drops when balanced and opponents are deep, combining with other placement options.
• Guardrail / exception: If the shot fails, investigate choice before assuming only contact mechanics.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional touch practice near the net with a partner.

16. Second serve — Segundo saque

Evidence: Direct · References: S, R

• Situation to recognise: Is it attacked because it is very soft, predictable or inaccurate?
• Basic — Notice: Notice where the second serve lands and the reply opponents make.
• Intermediate — Act: Use repeatable rhythm with a reliable depth/placement rather than pushing a very soft ball.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adapt safer targets to returner tendencies without trading away double-fault reliability.
• Guardrail / exception: The second serve remains subject to the same service rules.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional between-game target practice at comfortable pace.

Defensive shots

17. Return of serve — Resto

Evidence: Direct · References: E, Q, R

• Situation to recognise: Is the troublesome serve fast, central, or heading towards side glass?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you miss on the initial bounce or while waiting for glass.
• Intermediate — Act: Against difficult serves, get a safe return in; on easier serves choose purposeful depth/lob/low reply.
• Advanced — Adapt: Vary lob versus low return based on server’s movement and partner position.
• Guardrail / exception: Never require an attacking chiquita off every serve; serve return rules differ from rally rules.
• Optional practice (only when requested): If asked, compare central versus side-glass serve returns with a partner.

18. Forehand — Derecha

Evidence: Indirect · References: M, A

• Situation to recognise: Is the error linked to ball height, rushed contact or attempted pace?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether the forehand error occurs while stretched at the back.
• Intermediate — Act: Prioritise an in-court controlled reply from defensive positions rather than driving a low-percentage winner.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use direction and height to build points and create a better transition chance.
• Guardrail / exception: Specific racket mechanics here are insufficiently sourced; no confident diagnosis from text.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional comfortable feed and large-target placement practice.

19. Backhand — Revés

Evidence: Indirect · References: M, A

• Situation to recognise: Is the error on fast body balls, low contact or after glass?
• Basic — Notice: Observe whether your backhand is rushed or contact feels cramped.
• Intermediate — Act: From the back, choose controllable placement and time to recover rather than immediate power.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use depth and selected angles to move opponents and seek a more comfortable next ball.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not assume one- or two-handed grip must change without direct evidence.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional controlled backhand feeds with normal match-ready stance.

20. Defensive lob — Globo

Evidence: Direct · References: K, Q, P

• Situation to recognise: Is it short because the player is stretched, rushed, or aiming too low?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether short lobs follow off-balance contact.
• Intermediate — Act: Lob high and deep when balanced; when rushed, choose the safer achievable reply.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust height/depth to opponents’ overhead quality and recover with partner when lob creates time.
• Guardrail / exception: Not every defensive ball must be lobbed; a high but short lob can be attacked.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional aim at a generous deep target when court time allows.

21. Back glass return — Salida de pared

Evidence: Direct · References: G, M

• Situation to recognise: Is the ball too low, too high, or jamming the player after rebound?
• Basic — Notice: Notice which balls give enough time to use the back glass rather than block.
• Intermediate — Act: Prepare before the rebound and create space; contact in front when possible on a playable rebound.
• Advanced — Adapt: Choose defensive low return or controlled bajada according to rebound height, balance and opponents.
• Guardrail / exception: Never use fixed ‘always sideways/backwards’ movement or fixed starting distance.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional call ‘block/glass’ on variable feeds before playing.

22. Side glass return — Salida de lateral

Evidence: Indirect · References: D, G

• Situation to recognise: Is it the rebound direction, speed, or late contact that surprises the player?
• Basic — Notice: Observe whether fast or slow side-glass balls cause the error.
• Intermediate — Act: Decide early whether a readable rebound can be played after glass, or whether a pre-glass return is safer.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust position to the actual rebound angle and use safe replies when under pressure.
• Guardrail / exception: Avoid a universal turning technique or contact point for every side-glass ball.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional varied-angle feeds when feasible.

23. Double glass — Doble pared

Evidence: Direct · References: D

• Situation to recognise: Which surface does the ball touch first, and how sharply does it rebound?
• Basic — Notice: First recognise back-then-side versus side-then-back patterns.
• Intermediate — Act: For a readable rebound, watch through the second wall and allow room to meet the ball in front.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adapt turn/no-turn and spacing to rebound sequence and speed.
• Guardrail / exception: No ‘always rotate 360 degrees’; guidance for back-side may not fit side-back.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional slow corner feeds with clear calls of wall sequence.

24. Back wall rescue — Contrapared

Evidence: Limited · References: R, G

• Situation to recognise: Is a normal return still playable, or is the ball already behind the player?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise contrapared as an emergency option, not an everyday choice.
• Intermediate — Act: Prefer a controlled normal rebound return when available.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use legal own-glass recovery only when the normal route is unavailable and conditions permit.
• Guardrail / exception: Legal contact with own glass differs from mesh; avoid detailed speculative technique.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Only coach-led safe practice if wanted.

25. Defending a smash — Defensa del remate

Evidence: Direct · References: X

• Situation to recognise: Is the ball rebounding from back glass, side glass, or staying low?
• Basic — Notice: Notice which smashes give time for a rebound rather than instinctive block.
• Intermediate — Act: Against a suitable powerful smash, consider the rebound instead of charging forward to block.
• Advanced — Adapt: Anticipate rebound direction and opponent recovery; choose block, rebound or concede safely by situation.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not advise waiting for the glass on all smashes.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional coach/partner-assisted varied smash feeds; avoid unsafe high-speed practice.

26. Fence rebound — Rebote de reja

Evidence: Limited · References: R, A

• Situation to recognise: Did it strike wire mesh and deflect unpredictably?
• Basic — Notice: Notice that mesh rebound is not as predictable as smooth glass.
• Intermediate — Act: Keep a balanced ready position and adapt to the actual deflection instead of committing early.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use opponent patterns and court conditions to reduce rushed reaches after mesh contact.
• Guardrail / exception: Specific fence-reading technique lacks strong directly verified sources.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional gentle feeds from safe distance, not unsafe dives.

Transition shots

27. Chiquita — Chiquita

Evidence: Direct · References: C, Q

• Situation to recognise: Does it arrive at feet low, or sit up for an easy volley?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether a slow low ball forces opponents to volley upwards.
• Intermediate — Act: On a comfortable ball, aim a controlled low chiquita; advance only when opponent’s response creates time.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use down-line/cross-court placements to influence pair positioning and anticipate the next ball.
• Guardrail / exception: A chiquita is not an automatic invitation to rush the net.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional lower-pace target practice with partner.

28. Attacking lob — Globo de ataque

Evidence: Direct · References: K, P

• Situation to recognise: Are the opponents forward and can you hit a balanced, deep lob?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether the lob actually passes their overhead reach.
• Intermediate — Act: Use a controlled deeper lob when balanced to push opponents off the net.
• Advanced — Adapt: Vary lob direction and height to exploit players who retreat slowly while advancing with partner only if time allows.
• Guardrail / exception: Deep is relative to opponents’ reach; a poor lob can be attacked.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional match-only focus: note success on balanced lobs.

29. Passing shot — Passing

Evidence: Indirect · References: P, Q

• Situation to recognise: Are opponents covering net comfortably or displaced wide?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether attempts to pass come against a well-set pair.
• Intermediate — Act: Use lob or low ball at feet when net pair are set; pass only when a lane is genuinely open.
• Advanced — Adapt: Exploit movement patterns and unexpected space but manage risk versus the lob alternative.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not teach ‘passing winner’ as default transition method.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional situational practice against a net pair.

30. Half volley — Media volea

Evidence: Indirect · References: A, F

• Situation to recognise: Are you moving into the contact or stable on a low quick bounce?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you arrive late and contact while running.
• Intermediate — Act: On a low ball at the feet, favour a small controlled response and recover your balance.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use half volley to maintain transition only when time/position allows; otherwise retreat for a safer reply.
• Guardrail / exception: Not every ball at feet should be half-volleyed aggressively.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional gentle low-bounce contact practice on court.

Specialist shots

31. Between the legs — Entre piernas

Evidence: Limited · References: A

• Situation to recognise: Is the ball irretrievably behind the player, or is normal recovery possible?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise this as improvisation, not expected core proficiency.
• Intermediate — Act: Choose normal turning and recovery when possible rather than risky show shots.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use only when there is no practical normal contact and surrounding players are safe.
• Guardrail / exception: No source-backed step-by-step technique; avoid unsafe improvisation.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Coach-supervised optional fun shot only.

32. Behind the back — Por detrás de la espalda

Evidence: Limited · References: A

• Situation to recognise: Is the shot truly required by the ball’s position?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether a conventional reach or turn was available.
• Intermediate — Act: Prefer an ordinary controlled return to a blind behind-back contact.
• Advanced — Adapt: Only attempt if already skilled and there is space and a realistic recovery target.
• Guardrail / exception: Low evidence; do not claim this is necessary at high levels.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Coach-supervised optional novelty practice only.

33. Playing outside the court — Salida de pista

Evidence: Limited · References: R, A

• Situation to recognise: Is outside play authorised and the exit path safe?
• Basic — Notice: Know whether the court/match permits outside play; do not chase without awareness.
• Intermediate — Act: Prioritise safe stopping and the legal rally situation rather than an unsafe chase.
• Advanced — Adapt: Where legal and practised, anticipate x3 recovery path and opponent continuation options.
• Guardrail / exception: Must follow FIP and local venue rules; no improvised hazardous dives.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Coach-supervised situational rehearsal with safe exits only.

Movement

34. Knowing where to stand — Posición en pista

Evidence: Direct · References: T, P

• Situation to recognise: Is the player stuck in transition or disconnected from their partner?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you are at the net, back or stranded between positions when opponents hit.
• Intermediate — Act: Move into a coherent pair position after your shot, rather than drifting forward automatically.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust depth/width relative to ball and partner to close opponent’s likely angles.
• Guardrail / exception: No universal ‘always stand X metres from net’.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional review positioning with partner at changeovers.

35. Ready position and split step — Posición de espera y split step

Evidence: Direct · References: F, V

• Situation to recognise: Are you still running or flat-footed at the opponent’s hit?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you are ready when the opponent contacts the ball.
• Intermediate — Act: Use a light preparatory split at opponent contact, then move efficiently toward the ball.
• Advanced — Adapt: Scale split-step and recovery size with pace, terrain and fatigue; conserve movement without losing readiness.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not demand a big jump every point or imply fatigue is laziness.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional shadow split-step timing during warm-up.

36. Taking and holding the net — Subir y mantener la red

Evidence: Direct · References: P, T

• Situation to recognise: Did your previous ball create time or leave opponents an easy attack?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you advanced after a weak shot and were passed.
• Intermediate — Act: Advance with partner after a lob or low shot that genuinely pressures opponents; otherwise hold/reset.
• Advanced — Adapt: Alternate approach, hold and retreat decisions depending on opponent’s shot posture and pair shape.
• Guardrail / exception: Never prescribe ‘rush net after every lob/chiquita’.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional observe opponent quality before moving in during a social set.

37. Recovery — Recuperación

Evidence: Direct · References: B, T

• Situation to recognise: Did you watch your shot land or move back into court position?
• Basic — Notice: Notice the space you leave after volleys and overheads.
• Intermediate — Act: After contact, recover to cover the next likely shot with partner rather than admiring the ball.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust recovery angle/depth based on your placement and opponents’ available replies.
• Guardrail / exception: Recovery is not always ‘run straight to the net’.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional film one rally to see recovery positions.

38. Moving as a pair — Moverse en pareja

Evidence: Direct · References: T

• Situation to recognise: Does one stay forward as the other retreats, opening a diagonal?
• Basic — Notice: Notice your pair’s distance and shared movement as a rally changes.
• Intermediate — Act: Move laterally and forward/back together when the tactical situation calls for it.
• Advanced — Adapt: Cover the middle when partner is drawn wide; allow situational exceptions for reachable overheads.
• Guardrail / exception: Do not force both players backwards for every lob.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional talk through two court situations at changeover.

Tactics and teamwork

39. Shot choice — Elección del golpe

Evidence: Direct · References: O, P, Q

• Situation to recognise: Are errors from technique or trying high-risk shots on low-quality balls?
• Basic — Notice: After an error, ask whether the ball was comfortable to attack.
• Intermediate — Act: From difficult positions, choose a reliable reset; attack when balanced and the ball permits.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adapt control, target and tempo to opponents’ positioning and recurring habits.
• Guardrail / exception: No ‘one best shot’ for all situations.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional informal note: errors from choices vs errors from execution.

40. Talking to your partner — Comunicación

Evidence: Direct · References: T

• Situation to recognise: Are calls absent, late, contradictory or discouraging?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether the partner knows who will take a ball.
• Intermediate — Act: Agree one early clear call for uncertain balls and exchange useful opponent-position information.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use concise consistent calls to coordinate shifting, retreat and net approaches.
• Guardrail / exception: No rigid claim that all middle balls belong to one player.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional before-match agreement on one call each.

41. Building points — Construir el punto

Evidence: Direct · References: P, N

• Situation to recognise: Do attempted winners come from uncomfortable positions?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether you tried to finish on a setup ball.
• Intermediate — Act: Use one controlled placement to create a weaker next reply rather than forcing an immediate winner.
• Advanced — Adapt: Develop combinations that move opponents and manage pace/height with your partner.
• Guardrail / exception: Don’t assume passive always beats attacking or vice versa.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional choose one recurring two-shot pattern to observe.

42. The middle ball — Responsabilidad del centro

Evidence: Direct · References: T

• Situation to recognise: Does nobody call it, both chase it, or is there a positional gap?
• Basic — Notice: Notice where each player was when the middle ball arrived.
• Intermediate — Act: Agree early responsibility based on reach and coverage, with a clear call.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust who covers middle according to formation, opponent angle and pair movement.
• Guardrail / exception: No automatic ‘first mover owns it’ or ‘forehand always owns it’.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional agree communication before next set.

43. Rules and scoring — Reglas y tanteo

Evidence: Direct · References: R

• Situation to recognise: Is the question about official rule, club format or tactical score awareness?
• Basic — Notice: Recognise official scoring versus local match formats.
• Intermediate — Act: Check the official rule before claiming a point/let/fault; confirm event scoring format.
• Advanced — Adapt: Use score context to choose appropriate risk without assuming pressure causes mistakes.
• Guardrail / exception: Rules are factual, not a skill diagnosis; don’t invent or conflate local scoring alternatives.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional discuss serving order/tie-break with partner before play.

Mentality

44. Composure — Calma bajo presión

Evidence: Indirect · References: Y, W

• Situation to recognise: Did player describe nerves, or only report an important score?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether pace, grip or decision changes during important points.
• Intermediate — Act: If personally helpful, choose a simple between-point routine and a reliable target.
• Advanced — Adapt: Keep the same tactical process at big points; adjust only to observed opponent patterns, not the score alone.
• Guardrail / exception: Never infer anxiety solely from a missed break point.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional practise between-point routine during a casual set.

45. Bouncing back — Recuperarse del error

Evidence: Indirect · References: W, Y

• Situation to recognise: Does one mistake influence next decision or partnership communication?
• Basic — Notice: Notice whether thoughts from the previous point continue into the next.
• Intermediate — Act: After an error, acknowledge it and choose one simple intention for the next point.
• Advanced — Adapt: Agree partner language that helps reset rather than assigning blame, and protect enjoyment.
• Guardrail / exception: No mandatory positivity or diagnosis of mood.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional reflect on one reset that felt helpful.

46. Focus — Concentración

Evidence: Direct · References: J, Y

• Situation to recognise: Does focus fade with fatigue, distraction, or technical overthinking?
• Basic — Notice: Notice when attention leaves the ball or tactical situation.
• Intermediate — Act: Use one simple attention cue during rally; save technical analysis for between points.
• Advanced — Adapt: Adjust pre-point target and focus routine if tired, keeping in-rally attention on play.
• Guardrail / exception: Research on fatigue groups cannot diagnose an individual; do not demand constant analysis.
• Optional practice (only when requested): Optional short between-point reset routine to test.
