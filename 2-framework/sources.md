**There is no published, validated adult skill-by-level framework for padel.** Federations (FIP, LTA) publish rules and coaching courses, not player level descriptors. Level scales come from clubs and apps. Research describes what happens in matches, mostly at pro level. So Padel IQ has to combine practitioner scales, research findings, and coach review.

## What the evidence supports in our framework

- **Consistency first at lower levels.** Amateurs lose more points through unforced errors; pros win more with winners (Fernández-de-Osso et al., 2025). This supports the coach focus at D and C: get the ball back, then aim.
- **Net play, volley and bandeja as the core.** In pro men's padel, volleys are about 65% of attacking shots, bandejas about 23%, smashes about 12% (Sánchez-Alcaraz et al., 2020). Volleys, lobs and bandejas appear in most points (Martín-Miguel et al., 2024). This supports "Taking and holding the net" and the bandeja being central, with the smash as a finishing shot.
- **The lob as the way to the net.** About 60% of net approaches use the lob, at both advanced amateur and pro level (Muñoz et al., 2017). This supports our statements "After a good lob, I move up to the net" and "I win the net back on purpose".
- **Decision-making counts, not just technique.** A shot-accuracy test did not separate higher- and lower-level amateurs (Mendes et al., 2022). This supports having Tactics as its own area.
- **The order shots appear in.** Viya, Padel Nuestro and Bounce broadly agree with our order: basics, then lob and volley, then bandeja and glass, then víbora and chiquita, then smashes out of the court.
- **Teaching order.** TTOSP's experts: flat before spin, and play both sides before choosing one. Useful for practice suggestions.

## Where we differ, or have no backing

1. **Letter names clash with Dubai clubs.** At Viya, C is a wide band (levels 2–4), B is 4.5–5.5, and A means pro or top national player. Our A ("complete player") is about a Viya B or B+. A Dubai player who is "C at Viya" may be a Padel IQ B. **Action:** show a number next to each letter, or publish a mapping.
2. **Proposed mapping to check with a coach (Playtomic-style 0–7):** Beginner ≈ 0–1 · D ≈ 1–2 · C ≈ 2–3.5 · B ≈ 3.5–4.5 · A ≈ 4.5–5.5 · Pro ≈ 6+.
3. **Smashes out of the court (x3, x4).** We expect them at A. Viya expects them as a winning option at C+ (level 4), about our B. Consider moving them to B.
4. **Chiquita and drop shot.** Viya: chiquita understood at C, used well at C+; drop shots at C+. We have chiquita at C and drop shot at B. Roughly in line.
5. **Mentality area.** No level scale or study describes mentality by level for amateurs. It is reasonable coaching practice, but it is our own construct. Label it as such.
6. **Statements and warning questions.** All are drafts. No source has statement-level descriptors for adults. They need coach review.
7. **Pro research does not transfer directly.** Most match studies are on World Padel Tour players. Use them for "what matters", not for amateur level thresholds.

## How to make it valid (suggested method)

Copy the TTOSP method, which is standard content validation:

1. Ask 5–10 coaches to rate each statement for **relevance** (does it belong at this level?) and **clarity** (is the wording clear?) on a 1–4 scale.
2. Work out Aiken's V for each item. Keep items at about .70 or above, and revise or drop the rest using the coaches' comments.
3. Pilot with players whose level a coach knows. Check whether the quiz puts them at the same level.

The admin page (`?admin`) already has a review status and notes for each skill. A next step could be a coach rating mode that collects the 1–4 ratings and works out Aiken's V.
